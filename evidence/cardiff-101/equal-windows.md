# Cardiff 101: equal before and after windows

Oct 25, 2026. **OFFICIAL** state records only: SWITRS 2015 ([`data/switrs/`](../../data/switrs/README.md), Mark's TIMS export) plus CCRS 2016 to May 2026 ([`data/ccrs/`](../../data/ccrs/cardiff_101_cycle_track_bike_crashes.csv)). Stretch: south of Chesterfield Dr to the Solana Beach line. Crashes at the Chesterfield intersection itself are counted separately, as before.

## The comparison: 63 months each

| | Before: Jan 2015 to Mar 2020 | After: Apr 2020 to Jun 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Bike crashes on the stretch | **6** | **9** (10 counting one whose location needs checking) |
| Per year | 1.1 | 1.7 (1.9) |
| Hit a fixed object (wheel stop, post, curb) | **0** | **6** |
| Car turning across the rider | 2 | 0 |
| Car door or sideswipe | 2 | 1 |
| Rear-ended by a car | 1 | 0 |
| Other car crash | 0 | 1 (+1 to verify) |
| Hit a parked car | 1 | 0 |
| Rider hit a pedestrian | 0 | 1 |
| Fatal | 0 | **1** (Mar 2024) |
| Severe injury | 1 | 1 (+1 to verify) |
| Rider going north / south | 3 / 3 | 5 / 4 |

That's **50% more crashes per year** in the after window (67% if the uncertain one counts). The type of crash also changed completely: before, **no** rider hit a fixed object; after, **two-thirds** of crashes were riders hitting a fixed object.

Not in the 63-month after window (Jul 2025 to May 2026): one more on-stretch crash (Sep 29, 2025, car broadside, serious injury). With the full after period: 11 crashes in 74 months, 1.8 per year.

**Chesterfield intersection (counted separately):** 3 before (2016, 2018, 2019), 0 in the after window. The May 25, 2025 crash there (appearing as two reports an hour apart, probably one crash) falls just after the window.

## What a critic will say (and it's fair)

- **The before crashes were mostly cars:** right and left hooks, dooring, sideswipes. The track has removed most car conflicts from the official record (5 crashes involving a car before, 2 after). The trade was car crashes for crashes into the track's own hardware, and the after window has more crashes overall plus the only death.
- **Small numbers.** 6 versus 9 could partly be chance. The fixed-object shift (0 to 6) is the strongest signal, because it's a change in *kind*, not just count.
- **No ridership counts.** If many more people ride there now, more crashes could come from more riders, not more risk per rider. No counts exist either way.
- **Solo crashes are underreported** before and after. The advocates' lists (36 to 42 since 2020) suggest the official after count is far too low. We don't know how many were missed before.
- **The fatal crash** has bicycling under the influence as its primary factor.
