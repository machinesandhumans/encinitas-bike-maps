# Research and design guidance

Studies and official design guidance on the design features at issue: two-way tracks, vertical separators (wheel stops, posts, curbs), entries and exits, driveway and side-street crossings, roundabouts. Each item says which corridor it bears on.

## Supports the concern

| Year | Source | Finding | Bears on | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 (pub. 2020) | **RESEARCH** Cicchino et al., IIHS with GWU, OHSU, NYU. *Traffic Injury Prevention*: "Not all protected bike lanes are the same." 604 ER patients, DC, NYC, Portland | Crash-or-fall risk on **street-level two-way protected lanes was much higher than on major roads** (relative risk about 11, very wide interval). One-way protected lanes differed little from major roads. In protected lanes, collisions with vehicles happened **most often at intersections and at driveway and alley crossings**. Downhill grades also raised risk. | Cardiff (driveways, entries; see note), Santa Fe (driveways) | [IIHS release](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/some-protected-bike-lanes-leave-cyclists-vulnerable-to-injury) (checked) · [bibliography](https://www.iihs.org/topics/bibliography/ref/2193) |
| 2013 | **RESEARCH** Thomas & De Robertis, "The safety of urban cycle tracks: a review of the literature," *Accident Analysis & Prevention* 52:219-227 | "One-way cycle tracks are generally safer at intersections than two-way"; cycle tracks on busy streets reduce collisions **when suitable intersection designs are used**. | All four (intersection and driveway design) | [SWOV entry](https://swov.nl/nl/publicatie/safety-urban-cycle-tracks) (checked) |
| 2012 | **RESEARCH** Schepers & Klein Wolt, "Single-bicycle crash types and characteristics," *Cycling Research International* 2:119-135 | About **half of single-bicycle crashes are infrastructure-related**: hitting an obstacle or riding off the path, usually after the rider unintentionally takes a bad line. This is the crash type the state database most often misses. | Cardiff (6 of 11 official after-crashes hit a fixed object) | [SWOV](https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/single-bicycle-crash-types-and-characteristics) (checked) · [related: what cyclists need to see](https://swov.nl/en/publicatie/what-do-cyclists-need-see-avoid-single-bicycle-crashes) |
| 2009 | **RESEARCH** Daniels, Brijs, Nuyts & Wets, "Injury crashes with bicyclists at roundabouts," *Journal of Safety Research* 40(2):141-148. 90 roundabouts, Flanders, before/after | Bike injury crashes rose 93% at roundabouts with **painted cycle lanes** (CMF 1.93). **Severe** bike crashes rose at every design: lanes +37%, separated paths +42%, grade-separated +31%. Roundabouts replacing signals did worst. | Leucadia, Quail Gardens | [FHWA CMF Clearinghouse](https://cmfclearinghouse.fhwa.dot.gov/study_detail.php?stid=199) (checked) |
| 2022 | **GOV** Caltrans DIB 89-02, *Class IV Bikeway Guidance* (Feb 7, 2022) | Wheel stops are allowed as separators (§3.1) at 9 to 12 ft spacing (§3.2). Designers should consider "the compatibility of the barrier with clear recovery zone" requirements (§3.1); sight distance "should be evaluated at crossings of intersections, driveways, alleys" (§3.1); separation is dropped at driveways (§2.2); two-way bikeways concentrate riders and need controls where riders come from both directions (§2.0). Clear width 7 ft preferred, 5 ft minimum (§3.4). Entry tapers 10:1 to 5:1 preferred, 3:1 minimum (§3.5). | Cardiff (wheel stops, widths, entry tapers), Santa Fe | [PDF](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/design/documents/dib-89-02-final-a11y.pdf) (checked) |
| 2015 | **GOV** FHWA, *Separated Bike Lane Planning and Design Guide* | Federal guide covering one- and two-way layouts, driveways, transitions and intersections. Needs a page-by-page read for quotable lines on driveways and two-way conflicts. | All | [PDF (NACTO copy)](https://nacto.org/wp-content/uploads/FHWA-Separated-Bike-Lane-Planning-and-Design-Guide-2015.pdf) · [FHWA chapter pages](https://highways.dot.gov/safety/pedestrian-bicyclist/safety-tools/pg-83-88-separated-bike-lane-planning-and-design-guide) (not yet read) |
| 2022 | **GOV** California Traffic Control Devices Committee, item 21-22 (green pavement and bikeway posts) | State committee item on bikeway posts. Not read yet. | Cardiff | [PDF](https://dot.ca.gov/-/media/dot-media/programs/safety-programs/documents/ctcdc/item-21-22-green-pavement-bikeway-posts-050522-a11y.pdf) |
| 2024 | **ADVOCATE** CyclingSavvy, "How to ruin a buffered bike lane" | Argues posts and stops turn a usable buffer into a hazard. Opinion, not a study. | Cardiff | [link](https://cyclingsavvy.org/2024/06/how-to-ruin-a-buffered-bike-lane/) |

Note on the IIHS result and Cardiff: Cardiff has one-way tracks on each side (NB and SB), not a single two-way track, so the IIHS two-way finding applies to Cardiff only where riders use a track against traffic. Its driveway and intersection findings apply fully. Santa Fe's south side is a raised lane, which IIHS found *lower* risk (see below).

## Cuts the other way

These are the studies a city engineer or the Bicycle Coalition will cite. Know them.

| Year | Source | Finding | How to answer | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019 | IIHS (same study) | Two-way lanes **on bridges or raised from the roadway** had much *lower* risk than major roads. | Santa Fe's raised lane fits this; argue its driveway and side-street crossings instead. | [IIHS](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/some-protected-bike-lanes-leave-cyclists-vulnerable-to-injury) |
| 2019 | Peter Furth (Northeastern) critique of IIHS | The "11x" two-way result rests mostly on one five-block downtown stretch of DC's 15th St (10 of 21 cases); remove it and risk is about 1. Counting only collisions with vehicles, two-way protected lanes had relative risk 0.73, lower than all but off-street trails. | Concede the headline number is fragile. Note Furth's own point: most of the excess was falls and non-vehicle crashes at a stretch dense with driveways and crossings, which is the Cardiff pattern (fixed objects, entries). | [Furth blog](https://peterfurth.sites.northeastern.edu/2019/10/07/are-2-way-cycle-tracks-unsafe-a-closer-look-at-the-iihs-study) (checked) |
| 2012 | Teschke et al., *American Journal of Public Health* 102(12):2336-2343, Vancouver and Toronto case-crossover | Cycle tracks had the lowest injury risk of any route type: odds ratio 0.11 (95% CI 0.02 to 0.54), about one-ninth of major streets with parked cars. | Based on only 2 injury sites vs 10 control sites; wide interval. Cardiff's track has wheel stops and driveways that Teschke's tracks may not. | [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3519333) (checked) |
| 2011 | Lusk et al., "Risk of injury for bicycling on cycle tracks versus in the street," *Injury Prevention* 17:131-135, Montreal | Relative risk of injury on cycle tracks 0.72 (95% CI 0.60 to 0.85) vs comparable streets. | Montreal tracks are mostly two-way and curb- or median-separated; they show a well-built track can be safer. The question is whether Cardiff's is well built (separator type, entries, driveways). | [abstract](https://injuryprevention.bmj.com/content/17/2/131) (checked) |
| 2019 | Marshall & Ferenchak, "Why cities with high bicycling rates are safer for all road users," *Journal of Transport & Health*. 13 years, 12 US cities | Cities with more **protected and separated** bike infrastructure had lower fatality and serious injury rates for all road users. | City-level, not corridor-level; doesn't speak to wheel stops or entries. | [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2019/05/190529113036.htm) · [TRID](https://trid.trb.org/View/1629271) (not read in full) |
| 2013 | Thomas & De Robertis (same review) | Cycle tracks on busy streets reduce collisions and injuries when intersections are designed well. | Agree; the case is about Encinitas's specific entries and driveway crossings. | above |
